Where I land

F, with one change: take G’s ground. Your six hold up better than I expected — a cool blue accent on a warm cream ground is a tension almost nobody ships, and it is the single thing keeping this off the standard SaaS-blue shelf. But in F the canvas #F9F0E0 and the card fill are close enough in value that cards stop reading as raised. G’s deeper cream #F3E8D2 fixes that without touching your hues.

One real decision left: is rose the right danger? #CC3A63 is friendly — it reads notice where a chest-pain alert wants now. That is a defensible product choice for a family app that should not frighten people, and a wrong one if the P1 has to cut through. Swapping it for #B3261E keeps everything else intact. Your call, and it is the only place on this page where the palette can actually cost someone something.

H is the most interesting and the most dangerous. Rose-led is the warmest register here, but promoting rose to the brand forces danger down to #8E1B33, and those two are hard to tell apart at a glance. In a product whose whole job is flagging, an accent that resembles the alert is a genuine flaw. Press strip all colour on H before deciding — it is the one that needs it.

I pairs with F for free. If dark mode ever happens, it is already drawn, and your cream becomes the body text rather than being thrown away.

On the pastel set (J): the honest reading is that it is an illustration palette, not an interface one — every value sits in the top fifth of the range, so there is nothing left to build hierarchy with. K is the version I would actually defend: same seven hues, same warmth, pulled down until they can carry text. If you like J, ship K.

And take the lime either way. #FAFFC4 at 17:1 behind ink is the best highlight value on this page, better than my marigold and better than your #FADA7A. It costs one token to adopt into whichever palette wins.

The greyscale button at the top is the acceptance test on every option: press it and every dose status, severity and step state must still be readable. The app already enforces that in code, and no palette gets to break it.
